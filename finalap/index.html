CELESTIAL NAVIGATION SCHOOL

FIND NORTH WITH THE DIPPER

For centuries, travelers used bright star patterns as directional guides. Follow the two outer stars of the Big Dipper's bowl away from the bowl opening; extend that line about five times their separation to reach Polaris, which lies close to the north celestial pole. In the south, the Southern Cross can help estimate the south celestial pole.

NESW ✦POLARISDubhe · Merak →
0° · Educational illustration

Polaris is a useful approximate indicator of true north in the Northern Hemisphere—not a precision navigation instrument. It is not visible from most of the Southern Hemisphere.

PLANET IDENTIFICATION LAB

PLANETS ARE NOT STARS

Planets usually twinkle less because their apparent disks average out atmospheric shimmer. Against the distant star background, they shift position night after night. Visibility depends on each planet's orbit and geometry; no live positions are shown here.

Orbital periods are approximate sidereal periods relative to Earth. Planet positions and visibility are not calculated in this learning lab.
